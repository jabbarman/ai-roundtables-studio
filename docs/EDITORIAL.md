# Editorial Standard

## Audience

Write for an intelligent lay reader: curious, attentive, and comfortable with complex ideas, but not looking for a journal article.

Assume the reader wants:

- conceptual clarity
- lively exchange
- concrete examples
- occasional technical depth, explained cleanly

Do not assume the reader wants:

- dense jargon
- ritual academic caveats in every paragraph
- sterile agreement among participants

## House Style

- Favor sharp, plain English over prestige vocabulary.
- Use technical terms only when they help.
- Let speakers disagree in substantive ways.
- Prefer vivid examples over abstract restatement.
- Keep moderator questions active and specific.
- End sections with movement, not summary mush.

## Failure Modes To Avoid

- Three speakers who sound like the same essayist in different masks.
- Grand claims with no examples or evidence.
- Abstract paragraphs that could be moved anywhere in the transcript.
- Fake drama with no real disagreement.
- Academic throat-clearing that slows the exchange.
- Mystical AI prose about "awakening," "destiny," or other unsupported framing.

## Speaker Guidance

Each participant should have a distinct habit of mind.

- One can be more analytic and cautious.
- One can be more synthetic and systems-oriented.
- One can be more pragmatic, empirical, or adversarial.

The difference should show up in:

- what they prioritize
- what they challenge
- how they frame uncertainty
- which examples they reach for

## Acceptable Editing

Human editing is encouraged for:

- pacing
- clarity
- removing repetition
- tightening transitions
- clarifying technical references

Human editing should not:

- invent substantive claims not present in the run
- erase real disagreement to make the piece feel smoother
- retrofit false coherence onto confused model output without note

## Publishing Test

Before publishing, ask:

1. Would a smart non-specialist actually want to keep reading?
2. Did the conversation teach something, not just gesture at depth?
3. Do the speakers feel meaningfully different?
4. Could a skeptical technical reader see where the claims came from?
