# Series One: Everyday AI Questions

Series One restarts the AI roundtables with current models and a clearer
listener-first structure. The earlier published pieces remain intact as the
project's pilot series.

## Editorial Arc

The series moves from personal judgement to institutional power:

1. **When Should You Trust an AI Answer?**
2. **Who Is Responsible When AI Gets It Wrong?**
3. **Should an AI Have to Explain Itself?**
4. **What Should AI Be Allowed to Know About You?**
5. **Does AI Make Us More Capable or More Dependent?**
6. **Who Should Control Powerful AI?**

The first and third episodes revisit the original abstention and explanation
questions in language that starts with the audience's experience rather than
AI-industry terminology.

## Episode Contract

Each episode should:

- open with a recognisable situation and establish disagreement quickly
- use the second round to test positions against counterexamples or changed stakes
- end with practical guidance and an honestly unresolved question
- define technical terms when first used and avoid unnecessary model jargon
- remain intelligible when heard once as audio

The moderator represents an informed non-specialist listener. The moderator may
request an example, challenge unexplained terminology, or restate the active
dispute, but should not become a fourth participant.

## Model Slate

The calibration episode uses the highest broadly comparable models currently
available to this project:

- OpenAI: `gpt-5.5`
- Anthropic: `claude-opus-4-8`
- Google: `gemini-3.1-pro-preview`

Keep this slate fixed for the series unless a provider withdraws a model or a
material limitation appears. Record any change rather than silently mixing
model generations within the series.

## Calibration Gate

Produce Episode 1 completely before running the remaining episodes. Review it
for speaker distinctiveness, lay clarity, substantive disagreement, factual
discipline, pacing, and audio intelligibility. Apply prompt changes before
batch production so the rest of the series shares a stable editorial baseline.
