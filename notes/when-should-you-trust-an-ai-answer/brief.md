# When Should You Trust an AI Answer?

## Editorial Aim

Give a non-specialist listener a practical way to decide how much reliance to
place on an AI response. Do not reduce the issue to "AI sometimes hallucinates."
Trust should depend on the stakes, whether the claim can be checked, what
information may be missing, and what action the answer encourages.

## Opening Situation

A traveller asks an AI whether they need a visa, receives a confident answer,
and discovers a contradictory government webpage. They are due to fly the next
morning. What, exactly, should make them trust one answer over the other?

## Desired Tensions

- A useful answer cannot carry a warning label after every sentence.
- Fluent language is not evidence, but neither is institutional authority
  automatically current or easy to interpret.
- Users need practical verification habits, while product makers still carry
  responsibility for how confidence and sources are presented.
- Low-stakes experimentation and high-stakes reliance require different rules.

## Examples To Invite

- substituting an ingredient in a recipe
- checking a visa, tax, or benefits rule
- interpreting a symptom or medication interaction
- summarising a document the user can inspect
- reporting a current event that may have changed recently

## Success Test

The closing synthesis should leave the audience with a memorable decision rule,
not merely the advice to "double-check everything."
