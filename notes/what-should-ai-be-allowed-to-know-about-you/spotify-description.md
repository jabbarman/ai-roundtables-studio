# Spotify Description Draft

How much should an AI be allowed to know about you?

A personal AI assistant can become far more useful when it remembers your
preferences and connects to your email, calendar, documents and past
conversations. But what happens when it turns a calendar entry into a health
inference, uses an old salary document for a new purpose, or quietly builds a
theory about your finances, relationships or future?

This episode asks where useful personalisation becomes surveillance. Should
permission make personal-data reuse acceptable? Should sensitive inferences be
off-limits even when a user clicks agree? Or should the product architecture
prevent broad retrieval unless the user deliberately brings information into
the task?

The discussion develops a practical rule for personal AI: use the least context
needed for a named task, make sources and memories visible, let people correct
and delete them, expire context by default, and never silently infer or store
sensitive traits.

The roundtable is informed by the NIST Privacy Framework, guidance from the UK
Information Commissioner's Office on AI, data minimisation, lawfulness and
inference accuracy, and OpenAI's documentation on memory controls.
