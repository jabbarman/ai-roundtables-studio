# What Should AI Be Allowed to Know About You?

## Editorial Aim

Give a non-specialist listener a practical way to decide what personal context
an AI should be allowed to use. Avoid treating privacy as a simple choice
between convenience and secrecy. The harder question is whether the person can
understand, limit, correct, and expire what the system knows or infers about
them.

## Opening Situation

A personal AI assistant is connected to someone's email, calendar, documents,
and past conversations. It usefully remembers dietary needs and work routines.
Then it mentions a medical appointment, infers that the person may be trying to
start a family, and quietly uses an old salary document when giving career
advice. The user never explicitly asked it to remember or infer those facts.

## Desired Tensions

- Personal context can make an assistant genuinely useful, but collecting more
  data than a task needs creates privacy and security risk.
- A fact volunteered for one conversation is not automatically permission to
  retain it or reuse it for another purpose.
- Inferred information can be more sensitive and less accurate than information
  a person deliberately supplied.
- A memory control is weak if users cannot see what is remembered, where it came
  from, how it changes an answer, or how to delete it fully.
- Consent alone cannot carry the whole burden when interfaces encourage broad
  access or the downstream uses are difficult to predict.
- Some context should expire by default; some should never be inferred or
  retained without an explicit request.

## Examples To Invite

- an assistant connected to email and calendar that notices a medical pattern
- a career assistant using an old salary document outside its original task
- a shopping assistant inferring pregnancy, debt, religion, or sexuality
- a workplace assistant where the user and the organisation have different
  powers over retention and access
- a temporary travel-planning task that needs location and budget only briefly

## Success Test

The closing synthesis should give the listener a usable rule: give an AI the
minimum context needed for the current purpose; require separate permission for
retention, new purposes, and sensitive inference; and insist on the ability to
inspect, correct, delete, and expire what it knows.
