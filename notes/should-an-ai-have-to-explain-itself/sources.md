# Source Packet: Should an AI Have to Explain Itself?

These sources support a discussion about explanation, evidence, contestability,
and the limits of visible reasoning. They should be used to create practical
distinctions rather than a specialist debate about model internals.

## NIST AI Risk Management Framework 1.0

- URL: https://www.nist.gov/publications/artificial-intelligence-risk-management-framework-ai-rmf-10
- Relevance: Frames transparency, explainability, interpretability,
  accountability, reliability, and human oversight as connected trustworthiness
  attributes.

## OpenAI Model Spec

- URL: https://model-spec.openai.com/
- Relevance: Distinguishes hidden reasoning from user-facing answers and
  explains why raw internal reasoning is not the same thing as useful
  transparency.

## OpenAI Reasoning Models Guide

- URL: https://platform.openai.com/docs/guides/reasoning
- Relevance: Describes reasoning models and reasoning summaries, giving a
  concrete product example of summary-style explanation rather than raw
  chain-of-thought exposure.

## Anthropic: Measuring Faithfulness in Chain-of-Thought Reasoning

- URL: https://www.anthropic.com/research/measuring-faithfulness-in-chain-of-thought-reasoning
- Relevance: Raises the risk that visible chains of thought may not faithfully
  reflect why an answer was produced.

## EU AI Act Overview

- URL: https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai
- Relevance: Provides a risk-based frame for transparency and user rights in
  high-risk AI systems, including contexts where people need notice and
  contestability.
