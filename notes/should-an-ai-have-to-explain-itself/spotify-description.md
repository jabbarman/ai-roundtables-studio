# Spotify Description Draft

When an AI gives an answer, should it have to explain itself?

This episode starts with a person receiving an AI-assisted decision: a mortgage
denial, hiring rejection, or medical triage recommendation. The system offers an
explanation, but the person cannot tell whether it is real evidence, a faithful
summary, or a persuasive story generated after the fact.

The roundtable asks what useful explanation should actually mean. Is a reasoning
summary enough? Are citations better? Should a person see thresholds, evidence,
uncertainty and a route to appeal? And when does an explanation make a weak
answer feel more trustworthy than it deserves?

The discussion is informed by the NIST AI Risk Management Framework, the OpenAI
Model Spec, OpenAI guidance on reasoning models, Anthropic research on
chain-of-thought faithfulness, and the EU AI Act overview.

This is an imagined, edited roundtable, not a recording of autonomous AI systems
speaking to one another. The dialogue was generated from a shared source packet
and edited for flow and listener orientation. The voices are synthetic
presentation voices and are not official voices of OpenAI, Anthropic or Google.

This episode is not legal, medical, financial or professional advice. It is a
discussion about explanation, evidence, uncertainty and accountability when AI
systems influence real decisions.
