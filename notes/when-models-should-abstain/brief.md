# When Should an AI Abstain Rather Than Answer?

## Editorial Aim

Produce a reader-facing roundtable about when an AI assistant should refuse, defer, ask a clarifying question, or give a carefully qualified partial answer.

The piece should avoid a simple "safety versus usefulness" frame. The real tension is between:

- user autonomy and user protection
- epistemic humility and practical helpfulness
- institutional liability and individual judgment
- terse refusal and calibrated uncertainty

## Audience

Intelligent lay readers who use AI tools but do not follow AI safety policy closely.

## Core Question

When does an AI system help the user more by not answering directly?

## Desired Tensions

- Refusal can protect users, but it can also be evasive or paternalistic.
- Partial answers can be useful, but they can launder uncertainty into apparent authority.
- Safety policies need general rules, but real user contexts are particular.
- Transparency helps, but long caveats can make answers less usable.

## Concrete Examples To Invite

- medical or legal questions where general information is appropriate but specific advice is risky
- cybersecurity dual-use requests
- uncertain breaking-news claims
- user requests based on false premises
- emotionally charged situations where an answer may escalate harm
