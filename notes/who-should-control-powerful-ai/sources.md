# Source Packet: Who Should Control Powerful AI?

These sources support a discussion about how authority over powerful AI can be
divided among developers, evaluators, governments, standards bodies, users,
markets, and international institutions. They illustrate existing governance
mechanisms without assuming that any current framework settles the question.

## EU AI Act: General-Purpose AI Obligations

- URL: https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/guidelines-obligations-general-purpose-ai-providers
- Relevance: The European Commission explains that providers of
  general-purpose AI models face documentation, copyright, and
  downstream-information duties. Providers of models with systemic risk face
  additional obligations covering evaluations, risk mitigation, serious
  incidents, and cybersecurity. This is a concrete example of binding,
  risk-tiered public oversight.

## NIST AI Risk Management Framework Core

- URL: https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/5-sec-core/
- Relevance: NIST treats governance as a continuous function across the AI
  lifecycle. It says independent review can improve testing and mitigate
  internal bias and conflicts of interest. Because the framework is voluntary,
  it also exposes the distinction between defining good practice and enforcing
  it.

## UK AI Security Institute Frontier AI Trends Report

- URL: https://www.aisi.gov.uk/frontier-ai-trends-report
- Relevance: The institute reports evidence from two years of government
  testing across domains including cyber, chemistry, and biology. It argues for
  rigorous independent evaluations and collaboration, while cautioning that
  evaluations are snapshots rather than complete forecasts of real-world
  impact.

## OECD AI Principles

- URL: https://www.oecd.org/en/topics/ai-principles.html
- Relevance: The principles call for transparency, traceability, human
  oversight, robustness, and ongoing risk management. They allocate
  accountability according to each actor's role, context, and ability to act,
  supporting distributed responsibility rather than a single universal
  controller.

## UN Independent International Scientific Panel on AI Preliminary Report

- URL: https://www.un.org/independent-international-scientific-panel-ai/en/preliminary-report
- Relevance: The panel brings together independent experts from every region to
  provide a common scientific assessment for governments. Its preliminary
  report warns that safeguards may not keep pace with capability growth. The
  panel demonstrates how international institutions can improve shared evidence
  while leaving lawmaking and enforcement to politically accountable bodies.
