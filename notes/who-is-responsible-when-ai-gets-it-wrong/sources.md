# Source Packet: Who Is Responsible When AI Gets It Wrong?

These sources support a discussion about responsibility across users,
developers, deployers, and institutions. They should be used to create practical
distinctions, not to turn the episode into a legal briefing.

## Moffatt v. Air Canada, 2024 BCCRT 149

- URL: https://www.canlii.org/en/bc/bccrt/doc/2024/2024bccrt149/2024bccrt149.html
- Relevance: A concrete consumer example where a company faced consequences
  after its chatbot gave misleading policy information.

## NIST Generative AI Profile

- URL: https://www.nist.gov/publications/artificial-intelligence-risk-management-framework-generative-artificial-intelligence
- Relevance: Frames generative AI risk management across the lifecycle,
  including information integrity, human over-reliance, evaluation, and
  organisational controls.

## OECD AI Principles

- URL: https://www.oecd.org/en/topics/sub-issues/ai-principles.html
- Relevance: States that AI actors should be accountable for proper functioning
  according to their roles, context, and the state of the art.

## EU AI Act Overview

- URL: https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai
- Relevance: Gives a risk-based frame that separates obligations for AI
  developers and deployers, especially around high-risk uses.

## FTC Artificial Intelligence Guidance

- URL: https://www.ftc.gov/industry/technology/artificial-intelligence
- Relevance: Shows how consumer protection concerns apply to AI claims,
  deceptive uses, and business responsibility for AI-enabled products.
