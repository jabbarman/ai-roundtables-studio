# Spotify Description Draft

Does using AI make us more capable, or merely more capable while the tool is
present?

This episode begins with a junior analyst whose AI-assisted work becomes faster,
cleaner and good enough to earn a promotion. Then a client challenges a figure,
the AI is unavailable, and the analyst cannot reconstruct the assumptions,
explain the calculation or tell whether the challenge is valid.

The roundtable asks where capability actually resides: in the person, in the
temporary partnership between person and AI, or in the organisation's wider
workflow. Should students and professionals regularly practise without AI? Is
the ability to detect errors enough, or must people retain the ability to
reconstruct the work? And when does an unaided test confuse genuine competence
with denying someone a useful or necessary access tool?

The discussion develops a practical rule: delegate execution, but do not sign
off on consequential work unless you can explain the assumptions, check the
result independently, and recover a rough version when the tool is gone.

The episode is informed by research on AI-assisted customer support, the jagged
technological frontier in knowledge work, critical thinking with generative AI,
AI tutoring and unaided learning, and UNESCO guidance on human-centred uses of
generative AI in education and research.
